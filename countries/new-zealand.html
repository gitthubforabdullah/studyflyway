Get to know New Zealand

New Zealand, also known as Aotearoa, is in the southwest Pacific. Its two main islands are the North Island and South Island, alongside many smaller islands. Wellington is the capital and Auckland is the largest city. Coastal settings, mountains and regional differences shape the experience of living and studying here.

A brief history of New Zealand

Māori are the Indigenous people of Aotearoa, descended from Polynesian voyagers who settled the islands before European arrival. Māori language, iwi identities and relationships with land are central to the country's history and contemporary culture.

  1. Polynesian settlement: Voyagers established communities that developed the distinct cultures and traditions of Māori society.
  2. 1840: Māori rangatira and representatives of the British Crown began signing Te Tiriti o Waitangi / the Treaty of Waitangi. Differences between the Māori and English texts are important to understanding its history.
  3. The nineteenth century: European settlement expanded. Conflict over authority and land, including the New Zealand Wars and land confiscation, had lasting effects on Māori communities.
  4. 1893: New Zealand became the first self-governing country where women won the right to vote in parliamentary elections.

Popular Universities in New Zealand

A selection from StudyFlyway’s university profiles, not a ranking or endorsement. Compare the actual course, campus and entry requirements.

Auckland

University of Auckland

The University of Auckland grew from Auckland University College, which opened in 1883. It offers undergraduate, taught postgraduate and research study across a broad academic range. The City Campus places many students near central Auckland, while programmes can use other locations, including Grafton. Check the campus attached to the qualification you intend to study.

View University
Dunedin

University of Otago

The University of Otago was founded in 1869 and began teaching in 1871. Dunedin is its main campus, with additional teaching and research activity in other New Zealand cities. Its academic divisions cover business, health sciences, humanities and sciences. Applicants should check the location and selection process for their intended qualification, particularly in health disciplines.

View University
Christchurch

University of Canterbury

The University of Canterbury was established in 1873 and is based at Ilam in Christchurch. Its qualifications span creative, scientific and professional fields, with undergraduate and postgraduate options. International applicants can compare direct entry with recognised pathways using the university’s current admission guidance. This profile concerns the New Zealand institution.

Working While Studying

Student work rights depend on visa conditions and current government regulations. Check restrictions, permitted activities and any placement rules before accepting work. Employment and income are not guaranteed; avoid relying on a future job to cover essential study costs.

Studying in New Zealand: FAQs

Is New Zealand suitable for international students?

New Zealand offers university options across the North and South Islands. Compare taught degrees with research pathways and investigate supervision, fieldwork or practical components where relevant. The campus and surrounding city are important parts of the study experience.

How much does it cost to study in New Zealand?

New Zealand tuition varies by qualification and subject. Confirm the international fee applicable to your specific degree and include compulsory charges. Living expenses depend on your city and lifestyle; build a personal budget from current university and housing information.

Are scholarships available?

Explore university entrance scholarships and postgraduate research funding. Check country eligibility for government schemes rather than assuming all international applicants qualify. Check each award’s current eligibility and dates.

What are the admission requirements?

Requirements vary by course and study level. Prepare academic records and check language evidence, prerequisites, references and any portfolio or research requirements.

Do I need IELTS?

Not necessarily. Check the course’s accepted tests and alternative qualifications. There is no single score or exemption that applies to every university.

When should I apply?

February/March and July for many programmes. Plan backwards from the actual course and scholarship deadlines, allowing time for documents and immigration processing.

Can international students work while studying?

Only where their immigration conditions permit it. Verify current government rules; do not assume employment or income is guaranteed.

How do I find universities?

Use the university cards and the country-filtered StudyFlyway directory, then check courses on the official provider websites.

What immigration permission should I research?

Student Visa. The requirement depends on your nationality, course and circumstances; use the official authority linked in this guide.

Are tuition fees the same at every university?

No. Fees depend on the course, provider, study level and fee status. Check the exact programme and intake.
