Get to know New Zealand

New Zealand, also known as Aotearoa, is in the southwest Pacific. Its two main islands are the North Island and South Island, alongside many smaller islands. Wellington is the capital and Auckland is the largest city. Coastal settings, mountains and regional differences shape the experience of living and studying here.

A brief history of New Zealand

Māori are the Indigenous people of Aotearoa, descended from Polynesian voyagers who settled the islands before European arrival. Māori language, iwi identities and relationships with land are central to the country's history and contemporary culture.

  1. Polynesian settlement: Voyagers established communities that developed the distinct cultures and traditions of Māori society.
  2. 1840: Māori rangatira and representatives of the British Crown began signing Te Tiriti o Waitangi / the Treaty of Waitangi. Differences between the Māori and English texts are important to understanding its history.
  3. The nineteenth century: European settlement expanded. Conflict over authority and land, including the New Zealand Wars and land confiscation, had lasting effects on Māori communities.
  4. 1893: New Zealand became the first self-governing country where women won the right to vote in parliamentary elections.

Major cities and student destinations

These cities offer different settings for daily life. This is a selection of destinations, not a population ranking or a complete list of places to study.

Auckland · Wellington · Christchurch · Hamilton · Dunedin · Palmerston North

Auckland — North Island

New Zealand's largest city has a harbour setting and a diverse metropolitan population.

For your study plans: Compare campuses and commuting routes carefully; the wider Auckland area covers many neighbourhoods.

Explore University of Auckland

Wellington — North Island

The national capital is a centre for government, cultural institutions and creative activity.

For your study plans: Check whether your course is based at Kelburn, Pipitea or another location before choosing housing.

Explore Victoria University of Wellington

Christchurch — South Island

Canterbury's main city combines established neighbourhoods with a city centre reshaped by earthquake recovery.

For your study plans: Compare suburban campus locations with city-centre accommodation and allow for daily travel.

Explore University of Canterbury

Hamilton — North Island

Hamilton is an inland city on the Waikato River, within the Waikato region.

For your study plans: Research campus life and transport options rather than assuming an Auckland-based daily commute.

Explore University of Waikato

Dunedin — South Island

Dunedin has a strong university identity and historic architecture in the Otago region.

For your study plans: Investigate accommodation near your faculty, its heating arrangements and the academic-year contract.

Explore University of Otago

Palmerston North — North Island

A regional centre in Manawatū with an established education and research presence.

For your study plans: Confirm that your Massey programme is taught here, since the university also has other campuses and study modes.

Explore Massey University

Before choosing your city

Remember that New Zealand's seasons run opposite to Pakistan's. Compare the local climate and campus rather than assuming every city offers the same experience. University orientation and Māori cultural learning opportunities can help you understand place names, welcomes and community life.

Compare your exact campus, housing contract, daily travel and total budget before committing. Use the study-budget checklist and compare university profiles.

Your starting checklist

  • Academic certificates and transcripts for your previous study.
  • Identity documents and the language evidence requested by the course.
  • References, a statement, portfolio, or research proposal where required.
  • A calendar for admission, scholarship, and document deadlines.
