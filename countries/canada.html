Get to know Canada

Canada spans North America from the Atlantic to the Pacific and north into the Arctic. Ottawa is the national capital. Its provinces and territories have distinct landscapes, communities and education systems. English and French are central to public life, but the language of a particular degree must be checked with its institution.

A brief history of Canada

First Nations, Inuit and Métis histories are essential to understanding Canada. Indigenous societies existed long before French and British colonial expansion. The country's development includes both migration and nation-building, and the lasting harms of colonial policies toward Indigenous peoples.

  1. Before Confederation: Indigenous nations developed diverse societies across the continent. European settlement later brought French and British colonies, trade, conflict and changing political boundaries.
  2. 1867: Confederation united Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia and New Brunswick in the new Dominion of Canada. Other provinces and territories joined or were created over time.
  3. 1949: Newfoundland joined Confederation, becoming the tenth province.
  4. 1982: The Constitution was patriated and the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms became part of Canada's constitutional framework.

Popular Universities in Canada

A selection from StudyFlyway’s university profiles, not a ranking or endorsement. Compare the actual course, campus and entry requirements.

Vancouver and Kelowna

University of British Columbia

UBC has two main teaching campuses: Vancouver and Okanagan in Kelowna. Both offer undergraduate and graduate study, but their degrees and campus experiences differ. Its academic range includes arts, sciences, applied sciences and professional subjects. Choosing a campus is an important part of choosing a UBC programme.

Toronto, Mississauga and Scarborough

University of Toronto

The University of Toronto offers study at St. George, Mississauga and Scarborough in the Greater Toronto Area. Founded in 1827, it teaches across a wide range of humanities, scientific and professional disciplines. Programme availability, academic organisation and housing differ across its three campuses, so compare the exact campus and admission category before applying.

View University
Montréal

McGill University

McGill is a public university founded in 1821. Its downtown campus sits below Mount Royal in Montréal, and its Macdonald campus provides a different setting in Sainte-Anne-de-Bellevue. Students can explore arts, sciences, professional subjects and research degrees. The bilingual city and programme-specific language requirements deserve attention when planning study.

English Language Requirements

For English-taught study in Canada, universities may accept IELTS, TOEFL, PTE or other qualifications. Required scores vary depending on the university, degree level and course. Check accepted test versions, skill minimums and validity before booking.

French-taught programmes may require French-language evidence instead. Check the programme’s teaching language first.

Student Life in Canada

Campus clubs and international offices can help you find academic and social support. Compare transit routes, winter travel and housing contracts in your chosen Canadian city. In bilingual settings, consider which languages you will use both in class and in everyday life.

Ask about student societies, academic support, accessible facilities and international orientation. Compare accommodation contracts and public transport before choosing where to live.

Working While Studying

Student work rights depend on visa conditions and current government regulations. Check restrictions, permitted activities and any placement rules before accepting work. Employment and income are not guaranteed; avoid relying on a future job to cover essential study costs.

Studying in Canada: FAQs

Is Canada suitable for international students?

Canada offers university study in English and French, with academic environments that vary across provinces. Compare the language of instruction, research interests and whether a course includes an optional or required work placement. Institutional admission and study permit eligibility need separate checks.

How much does it cost to study in Canada?

Canadian tuition depends on the institution, programme and international fee status. Include compulsory student fees and any placement-related expenses. Living expenses depend on your city and lifestyle; build a personal budget from current university and housing information.

Are scholarships available?

Investigate university entrance awards, need-based assistance where available and research funding. Some awards require nomination or a separate application. Check each award’s current eligibility and dates.

What are the admission requirements?

Requirements vary by course and study level. Prepare academic records and check language evidence, prerequisites, references and any portfolio or research requirements.

Do I need IELTS?

Not necessarily. Check the course’s accepted tests and alternative qualifications. There is no single score or exemption that applies to every university.

When should I apply?

Fall entry; winter or other starts where offered. Plan backwards from the actual course and scholarship deadlines, allowing time for documents and immigration processing.

Can international students work while studying?

Only where their immigration conditions permit it. Verify current government rules; do not assume employment or income is guaranteed.

How do I find universities?

Use the university cards and the country-filtered StudyFlyway directory, then check courses on the official provider websites.

What immigration permission should I research?

Study Permit. The requirement depends on your nationality, course and circumstances; use the official authority linked in this guide.

Are tuition fees the same at every university?

No. Fees depend on the course, provider, study level and fee status. Check the exact programme and intake.
