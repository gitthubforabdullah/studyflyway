Get to know the United Kingdom

The United Kingdom comprises England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. London is the UK capital, while Edinburgh, Cardiff and Belfast are the capitals of Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Great Britain refers to the island containing England, Scotland and Wales; it is not another name for the entire UK.

A brief history of the United Kingdom

The UK's history reflects several nations with distinct languages, institutions and traditions. Medieval kingdoms, parliamentary government, industrialisation, empire and migration all helped shape its cities. Studying in one part of the UK can therefore offer a different cultural and academic setting from studying in another.

  1. 1707: The union of England and Scotland created the Kingdom of Great Britain and a single Parliament at Westminster.
  2. 1801: Union with Ireland created the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland.
  3. The industrial era: Manufacturing and trade transformed cities such as Manchester, Birmingham and Glasgow. Industrial heritage remains visible in museums, canals and former factory districts.
  4. The twentieth century: Most of Ireland left the UK in 1922. The name United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland was adopted in 1927. Decolonisation and migration subsequently reshaped society.

History sources: UK Parliament: the United Kingdom and independence · British Council: the UK's nations.

Major cities and student destinations

These cities offer different settings for daily life. This is a selection of destinations, not a population ranking or a complete list of places to study.

London · Manchester · Birmingham · Edinburgh · Glasgow · Cardiff · Belfast

London — England

The UK capital brings together government, museums, theatres and universities across a large metropolitan area.

For your study plans: Compare the campus postcode, transport zones and accommodation cost rather than treating all London locations as equivalent.

Explore University College London

Manchester — England

A former industrial centre with a strong music, sport and cultural identity.

For your study plans: Check which university buildings you will use and compare housing by the journey to those buildings.

Explore University of Manchester

Birmingham — England

A major Midlands city whose manufacturing heritage sits alongside modern commercial and cultural districts.

For your study plans: Compare campus-based living with accommodation elsewhere in the city.

Explore University of Birmingham

Edinburgh — Scotland

Scotland's capital is known for its historic Old and New Towns and its festivals.

For your study plans: Check which campus serves your subject; university facilities extend beyond the historic centre.

Explore University of Edinburgh

Glasgow — Scotland

Glasgow's industrial and shipbuilding history is complemented by a prominent music, design and arts scene.

For your study plans: Investigate the neighbourhood around your teaching campus and local transport connections.

Explore University of Glasgow

Cardiff — Wales

The Welsh capital combines a civic centre, a castle and a redeveloped waterfront.

For your study plans: Explore Welsh cultural institutions and compare the location of your course with available housing.

Belfast — Northern Ireland

Northern Ireland's capital has a maritime and industrial heritage, including its connection with Titanic.

For your study plans: Read local history alongside your course research and confirm campus accommodation arrangements.

Your starting checklist

  • Academic certificates and transcripts for your previous study.
  • Identity documents and the language evidence requested by the course.
  • References, a statement, portfolio, or research proposal where required.
  • A calendar for admission, scholarship, and document deadlines.

Requirements vary by institution and course. Check the official programme page before preparing or paying for documents.
