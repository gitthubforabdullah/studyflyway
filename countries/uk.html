Get to know the United Kingdom

The United Kingdom comprises England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. London is the UK capital, while Edinburgh, Cardiff and Belfast are the capitals of Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Great Britain refers to the island containing England, Scotland and Wales; it is not another name for the entire UK.

A brief history of the United Kingdom

The UK's history reflects several nations with distinct languages, institutions and traditions. Medieval kingdoms, parliamentary government, industrialisation, empire and migration all helped shape its cities. Studying in one part of the UK can therefore offer a different cultural and academic setting from studying in another.

  1. 1707: The union of England and Scotland created the Kingdom of Great Britain and a single Parliament at Westminster.
  2. 1801: Union with Ireland created the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland.
  3. The industrial era: Manufacturing and trade transformed cities such as Manchester, Birmingham and Glasgow. Industrial heritage remains visible in museums, canals and former factory districts.
  4. The twentieth century: Most of Ireland left the UK in 1922. The name United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland was adopted in 1927. Decolonisation and migration subsequently reshaped society.

History sources: UK Parliament: the United Kingdom and independence · British Council: the UK's nations.

Popular Universities in the United Kingdom

A selection from StudyFlyway’s university profiles, not a ranking or endorsement. Compare the actual course, campus and entry requirements.

Manchester

University of Manchester

The current University of Manchester was formed in 2004 by merging the Victoria University of Manchester and UMIST, with predecessor roots extending to 1824. It brings together humanities, science and engineering, and biology, medicine and health. Its Oxford Road setting connects academic buildings with the wider city rather than a single enclosed residential campus.

Edinburgh

University of Edinburgh

The University of Edinburgh was founded in 1583 and teaches across three broad colleges: arts, humanities and social sciences; medicine and veterinary medicine; and science and engineering. Its buildings are spread across several parts of Edinburgh. The teaching location depends on the subject, so check where classes and any clinical or practical work take place.

View University
Birmingham

University of Birmingham

The University of Birmingham received its Royal Charter in 1900. Its Edgbaston campus is the focus of this UK profile, with academic study spanning the arts, sciences and professional disciplines. Applicants should distinguish UK courses from those offered at the university’s Dubai campus when checking admissions, fees and accommodation.

Student Life in the United Kingdom

Student unions and societies can provide a starting point for meeting people and joining campus activities. Compare university halls with private accommodation, and check transport between housing and teaching sites. Life in London can feel different from a smaller university town or a Scottish city.

Ask about student societies, academic support, accessible facilities and international orientation. Compare accommodation contracts and public transport before choosing where to live.

Working While Studying

Student work rights depend on visa conditions and current government regulations. Check restrictions, permitted activities and any placement rules before accepting work. Employment and income are not guaranteed; avoid relying on a future job to cover essential study costs.

Studying in the United Kingdom: FAQs

Is the United Kingdom suitable for international students?

The United Kingdom offers universities across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Course structures, assessment and degree lengths can differ, so compare the full curriculum rather than just the award title. Research-led study and specialist professional courses offer different routes for different goals.

How much does it cost to study in the United Kingdom?

UK course fees vary by subject, institution and fee status. Compare the cost of the whole qualification, particularly when considering programmes of different lengths. Living expenses depend on your city and lifestyle; build a personal budget from current university and housing information.

Are scholarships available?

Explore university scholarships, government-funded awards and research studentships. Check nationality, experience and subject restrictions for each funding scheme. Check each award’s current eligibility and dates.

What are the admission requirements?

Requirements vary by course and study level. Prepare academic records and check language evidence, prerequisites, references and any portfolio or research requirements.

Do I need IELTS?

Not necessarily. Check the course’s accepted tests and alternative qualifications. There is no single score or exemption that applies to every university.

When should I apply?

September/October; other starts on selected courses. Plan backwards from the actual course and scholarship deadlines, allowing time for documents and immigration processing.

Can international students work while studying?

Only where their immigration conditions permit it. Verify current government rules; do not assume employment or income is guaranteed.

How do I find universities?

Use the university cards and the country-filtered StudyFlyway directory, then check courses on the official provider websites.

What immigration permission should I research?

Student Visa. The requirement depends on your nationality, course and circumstances; use the official authority linked in this guide.

Are tuition fees the same at every university?

No. Fees depend on the course, provider, study level and fee status. Check the exact programme and intake.
