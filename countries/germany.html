Get to know Germany

Germany lies in central Europe and has a federal system of 16 states. Berlin is the capital. Large metropolitan areas sit alongside smaller university towns, so a well-matched course may be outside the best-known cities. German is important in everyday life even when a degree is taught in English.

A brief history of Germany

Modern Germany grew from a much older landscape of kingdoms, principalities and trading cities. Its history includes scientific and cultural achievement as well as dictatorship, war and the Holocaust. Historic sites and memorials are important places to understand how the country remembers its past.

  1. 1871: The German Empire was established, unifying many German states under a national government.
  2. 1919: After the First World War and the end of the monarchy, the Weimar Republic established a parliamentary democracy.
  3. 1933–1945: Nazi dictatorship destroyed democratic institutions, carried out the Holocaust and waged war across Europe. Its defeat in 1945 marked a fundamental rupture.
  4. 1949: Separate West German and East German states emerged from the post-war division.
  5. 1989–1990: The Berlin Wall opened in November 1989. German reunification followed on 3 October 1990.

History sources: Deutschlandmuseum: German history timeline · Federal Government: the path to reunification.

Major cities and student destinations

These cities offer different settings for daily life. This is a selection of destinations, not a population ranking or a complete list of places to study.

Berlin · Munich · Hamburg · Frankfurt am Main · Cologne · Stuttgart · Dresden

Berlin — Berlin

The capital combines national government, museums and creative districts with sites connected to Germany's division and reunification.

For your study plans: Compare campus addresses and housing across the city instead of relying on distance from the tourist centre.

Munich — Bavaria

Bavaria's capital is known for its museums, historic centre and proximity to the Alpine region.

For your study plans: Check whether classes take place in central Munich or at a separate campus such as Garching.

Explore Technical University of Munich

Hamburg — Hamburg

A northern port city whose harbour and trading history remain central to its identity.

For your study plans: Look at local transport connections and the location of your department when planning accommodation.

Explore University of Hamburg

Frankfurt am Main — Hesse

Frankfurt is associated with banking, an international airport and a distinctive high-rise skyline.

For your study plans: Distinguish Frankfurt am Main from Frankfurt an der Oder when researching institutions and travel.

Cologne — North Rhine-Westphalia

This Rhine city is known for its cathedral, Roman roots and cultural life.

For your study plans: Compare the daily campus journey as well as regional rail connections to surrounding cities.

Explore University of Cologne

Stuttgart — Baden-Württemberg

Stuttgart is associated with automobile manufacturing and a landscape of hills and valleys.

For your study plans: Check whether your course uses the city-centre or Vaihingen campus before arranging housing.

Dresden — Saxony

Dresden sits on the Elbe and is known for art collections and reconstructed historic architecture.

For your study plans: Combine academic research with a check of neighbourhoods, transport and student housing applications.

Munich and surrounding campus locations

Technical University of Munich

TUM was founded in Munich in 1868 and now operates across several locations. Its academic portfolio connects engineering and technology with natural sciences, life sciences, medicine, management and social questions. Munich and Garching are distinct teaching locations; the degree catalogue specifies the campus, language and admission process for each programme.

Heidelberg

Heidelberg University

Heidelberg University began teaching in 1386. Its subject range includes the humanities, social sciences, natural sciences, law and medicine. University buildings are distributed between the Old Town and other locations, including Neuenheimer Feld. Applicants should use the subject catalogue to identify the teaching location and language rather than assuming every degree is taught in English.

View University
Aachen

RWTH Aachen University

RWTH Aachen is a technical university whose history begins in 1870. Engineering and natural sciences form a substantial part of its academic range, alongside medicine, business and humanities. University facilities are spread across Aachen. The programme catalogue distinguishes degrees, admission conditions and teaching languages, which is especially important for international applicants.

Admission Requirements

To study in Germany from Pakistan, begin with the exact degree and teaching language. Prepare a shortlist that records academic prerequisites, the required German or English evidence, the application route and the deadline for each programme.

Ask admissions how your Pakistani school certificate or university degree will be assessed. For postgraduate study, keep transcripts and module descriptions ready so you can explain how your previous studies match the course. Use the official resources below to check recognition and document requirements before arranging translations or paying application fees.

DAAD’s admission resources can help you investigate qualification recognition. The university makes the final admission decision. Check whether your selected course requires German or English evidence and whether applications go directly to the institution or through a specified application service.

English Language Requirements

For English-taught study in Germany, universities may accept IELTS, TOEFL, PTE or other qualifications. Required scores vary depending on the university, degree level and course. Check accepted test versions, skill minimums and validity before booking.

German-taught programmes require the German evidence specified by the university; an English test does not replace it.

Tuition Fees

German fees depend on the university, programme and applicable rules. Check tuition and semester contributions separately; private or specialised programmes may have different charges.

Tuition fees vary significantly depending on the university, degree level and field of study. Confirm current tuition, payment dates and refund terms on the official university website before accepting an offer.

Student Life in Germany

Student services organisations and university international offices are useful starting points for housing and orientation. German language learning can help with everyday tasks even on an English-taught course. Compare public transport, shared housing and the location of your faculty.

Ask about student societies, academic support, accessible facilities and international orientation. Compare accommodation contracts and public transport before choosing where to live.

Working While Studying

Student work rights depend on visa conditions and current government regulations. Check restrictions, permitted activities and any placement rules before accepting work. Employment and income are not guaranteed; avoid relying on a future job to cover essential study costs.

Studying in Germany: FAQs

Is Germany suitable for international students?

Germany offers research universities and universities of applied sciences with different approaches to study. Compare theoretical content, practical components and the language of instruction. Programme fit and recognition of previous qualifications matter as much as the institution’s location.

How much does it cost to study in Germany?

German fees depend on the university, programme and applicable rules. Check tuition and semester contributions separately; private or specialised programmes may have different charges. Living expenses depend on your city and lifestyle; build a personal budget from current university and housing information.

Are scholarships available?

DAAD funding, university awards and research opportunities have different eligibility rules. Match the scholarship to the programme rather than assuming every German degree is funded. Check each award’s current eligibility and dates.

What are the admission requirements?

Requirements vary by course and study level. Prepare academic records and check language evidence, prerequisites, references and any portfolio or research requirements.

Do I need IELTS?

Not necessarily. Check the course’s accepted tests and alternative qualifications. There is no single score or exemption that applies to every university.

When should I apply?

Winter and summer semesters; course availability varies. Plan backwards from the actual course and scholarship deadlines, allowing time for documents and immigration processing.

Can international students work while studying?

Only where their immigration conditions permit it. Verify current government rules; do not assume employment or income is guaranteed.

How do I find universities?

Use the university cards and the country-filtered StudyFlyway directory, then check courses on the official provider websites.

What immigration permission should I research?

Student Visa and Residence Requirements. The requirement depends on your nationality, course and circumstances; use the official authority linked in this guide.

Are tuition fees the same at every university?

No. Fees depend on the course, provider, study level and fee status. Check the exact programme and intake.
