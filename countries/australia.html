Get to know Australia

Australia stretches from tropical northern regions to temperate southern cities and a vast inland interior. Canberra is the national capital; Sydney and Melbourne are separate state capitals. Distances between cities can be substantial, so your choice of campus shapes everyday life as much as your choice of university.

A brief history of Australia

Australian history begins with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples, whose distinct cultures and connections to land and sea long predate European settlement. Understanding this continuing heritage gives important context to place names, museums and university acknowledgements of Country.

  1. Before European settlement: Indigenous communities developed diverse languages, knowledge systems and trading connections across the continent and surrounding islands.
  2. 1788: The First Fleet established a British colony at Sydney Cove. Colonisation brought dispossession, conflict and profound disruption to Indigenous peoples.
  3. 1901: Six British colonies federated to form the Commonwealth of Australia. Federation created a national government while retaining state governments.
  4. The twentieth century: Migration and urban growth helped shape modern Australia's culturally diverse society. Indigenous rights and recognition remain important parts of its national story.

History sources: National Museum of Australia: defining moments · National Museum of Australia: Federation.

Popular Universities in Australia

A selection from StudyFlyway’s university profiles, not a ranking or endorsement. Compare the actual course, campus and entry requirements.

Melbourne

University of Melbourne

The University of Melbourne combines a broad undergraduate education with specialised graduate and research study. Its original Parkville campus sits close to central Melbourne, while other campuses serve areas such as fine arts, music and agricultural study. Students should compare the undergraduate major and any later professional qualification as separate stages of their study plan.

View University
Sydney

University of Sydney

Founded as a public institution in 1850, the University of Sydney offers study across the humanities, sciences and professional disciplines. Its Camperdown and Darlington campus brings historic sandstone buildings together with teaching and research facilities. Course location matters: some programmes use specialist campuses or clinical settings elsewhere.

View University
Melbourne

Monash University

Monash was established in 1958, with its first students beginning at Clayton in 1961. It now teaches across multiple Australian campuses and an international network. The Australian course catalogue spans scientific, creative and professional fields. Applicants should check both the degree and its teaching campus rather than treating every Monash location as interchangeable.

Student Life in Australia

Australian campuses may offer clubs, sport, academic support and international student services. Compare transport access and accommodation near your specific teaching campus. Conditions differ between tropical northern regions and cooler southern cities; plan clothing and outdoor activities around the local climate.

Ask about student societies, academic support, accessible facilities and international orientation. Compare accommodation contracts and public transport before choosing where to live.

Working While Studying

Student work rights depend on visa conditions and current government regulations. Check restrictions, permitted activities and any placement rules before accepting work. Employment and income are not guaranteed; avoid relying on a future job to cover essential study costs.

Studying in Australia: FAQs

Is Australia suitable for international students?

Australia offers research universities and a broad choice of academic and professional courses. Comparing a metropolitan campus with a regional one can reveal different class settings, travel needs and student communities. Investigate research groups and placement arrangements at course level rather than choosing on the country name alone.

How much does it cost to study in Australia?

Australian international course fees depend on the provider and subject. Check whether quoted fees cover a year, a study period or the full qualification. Living expenses depend on your city and lifestyle; build a personal budget from current university and housing information.

Are scholarships available?

Compare government awards with university merit scholarships and graduate research funding; nationality and degree-level eligibility differ. Check each award’s current eligibility and dates.

What are the admission requirements?

Requirements vary by course and study level. Prepare academic records and check language evidence, prerequisites, references and any portfolio or research requirements.

Do I need IELTS?

Not necessarily. Check the course’s accepted tests and alternative qualifications. There is no single score or exemption that applies to every university.

When should I apply?

February/March; July for selected courses. Plan backwards from the actual course and scholarship deadlines, allowing time for documents and immigration processing.

Can international students work while studying?

Only where their immigration conditions permit it. Verify current government rules; do not assume employment or income is guaranteed.

How do I find universities?

Use the university cards and the country-filtered StudyFlyway directory, then check courses on the official provider websites.

What immigration permission should I research?

Student Visa. The requirement depends on your nationality, course and circumstances; use the official authority linked in this guide.

Are tuition fees the same at every university?

No. Fees depend on the course, provider, study level and fee status. Check the exact programme and intake.
